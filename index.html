Act 3 · The solution (simulation)

Hear the whale. Locate it. Route around it.

Ships strike whales because nobody knows exactly where the whales are. A single listening buoy can say “a whale is somewhere within 20–100 km” — so today’s answer is a blanket slow zone. This simulation shows what a network of low-cost hydrophones changes: every call is heard by several buoys, the whale is located from the tiny differences in arrival time, and the ship makes a small, cheap, targeted change instead.

  1. Listen — thousands of buoys along the Oakland → Long Beach lanes, through real strike hotspots.
  2. Locate — arrival-time differences (like GPS in reverse) give a position and an error ellipse.
  3. Track — a Kalman filter follows each whale and predicts where it will be when the ship arrives.
  4. Decide — a transparent engine compares slowing down, shifting course and today’s practice on strike risk, noise, time, fuel, $ and CO₂ — every number traceable to a formula and a source.
